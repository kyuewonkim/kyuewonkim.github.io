Fading the Periphery: Peripheral Brightness Rolloff for Cybersickness Mitigation in VR

Kyuewon Kim, Uijong Ju

IEEE VR 2027 (Paper) · Submitted

Abstract

Cybersickness remains a major barrier to the widespread adoption of virtual reality (VR). Dynamic Field-of-view Restriction (DFR), one of the most widely used mitigation strategies, reduces symptoms by occluding peripheral vision, but the occlusion itself can be visually salient and reduce presence. As an alternative, we propose Peripheral Brightness Rolloff (PBR), a gaze-contingent technique that preserves scene structure while gradually attenuating peripheral luminance with increasing eccentricity from the user’s gaze. Unlike occlusion or blur, PBR reduces the visual salience of peripheral motion cues without removing scene information. To evaluate the proposed technique, we conducted a within-subjects experiment with 36 participants in a free-locomotion virtual environment, comparing Original, PBR, and DFR in terms of cybersickness, presence, subjective visual experience, and task performance. Both PBR and DFR significantly reduced cybersickness relative to Original. PBR also yielded significantly lower Nausea than DFR, with no significant differences in the remaining SSQ subscales. Presence was significantly higher with PBR than with DFR, and preference was significantly higher with PBR than with both Original and DFR. Recognition performance under PBR remained comparable to Original, whereas DFR significantly reduced performance. Overall, these findings indicate that PBR can reduce cybersickness while preserving presence and task performance, providing a viable non-occluding alternative to DFR.
