Functional connectivity correlates of subjective visual fatigue induced by visual content manipulation: A whole-brain fMRI study

Uijong Ju, Kyuewon Kim

Displays

Abstract

Visual fatigue caused by display devices is a growing concern that impacts quality of life. To investigate the neural mechanisms underlying this phenomenon, we manipulated the motion and brightness of video content while measuring task-based functional connectivity (tbFC) using fMRI. Twenty-nine participants viewed four videos across conditions varying in motion (high vs. low) and brightness (high vs. low), continuously rating their visual fatigue during viewing and providing overall ratings after each video. Behaviorally, high-motion videos induced significantly greater fatigue, while brightness had no significant effect. tbFC analyses revealed distinct connectivity patterns only for motion contrasts, with visual, motor, and default mode networks showing significant differences. Crucially, brain connectivity patterns significantly predicted subjective fatigue, especially under high-motion conditions. Predictive networks varied: under high-motion/low-brightness, motor and visual areas were key; under high-motion/high-brightness, frontal, parietal, and limbic networks dominated. These findings suggest tbFC as a potential neural marker for visual fatigue and may inform content-specific strategies to reduce fatigue in display-based environments.
